# 求名章·占小考须先占县考、再占府考、又占道考，都得吉卦才许榜上有名；内有一卦不吉，勿望道考成名（野鹤）

六爻 · 考试 · 小考 · 出处 [[增删卜易]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 占小考（县考、府考、道考这几关），神应近不应远，一卦吉只应在府县有名；所以要先占县考，再占府考，又占道考，三卦都吉才可以说榜上有名，有一卦不吉，就别指望道考成名。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* For the minor examinations (the county, prefecture and circuit examinations): the spirits answer what is near and not what is far, so one auspicious hexagram answers only for the name being on the county or prefecture list; one must divine the county examination first, then the prefecture, then the circuit, and only when all three are auspicious may one say the name will be on the list; if one of them is not auspicious, do not hope for fame at the circuit examination.

## 野鹤老人(《增删卜易》)

> [!quote]+ [[增删卜易]] 第 9916 行 · The Old Man of Yehe (Zengshan Buyi)
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 野鹤曰：或问凡占小考，尝见卜得吉卦，后竟道考无名者，何也？ | 野鹤说：有人问，凡是占小考，常常看到占得吉卦，后来竟然道考没有名字，这是为什么？ | The Old Man of Yehe says: someone asked, "In divining for the minor examinations I have often seen an auspicious hexagram obtained and then the name still did not appear at the circuit examination; why is that?" |
> | 余曰：神每应近而不应远，已吉卦者，乃应府县之有名也，故凡占小考者，必须先占县考，再占府考，又占道考，俱得吉卦者，许之榜上有名。 | 我说：神常常应近的、不应远的，已经得吉卦的，是应在府县有名；所以凡是占小考的，必须先占县考，再占府考，又占道考，都得吉卦的，才许他榜上有名。 | I said: the spirits usually answer what is near and not what is far; an auspicious hexagram already obtained answers that the name will be on the county and prefecture lists; so for the minor examinations one must first divine the county examination, then the prefecture examination, then the circuit examination, and only if all give auspicious hexagrams may one say that the name will be on the list. |
> | 内有一卦而不吉者，勿望道考而成名也。 | 其中有一卦不吉的，不要指望道考能成名。 | If any one of them is not auspicious, do not hope for fame at the circuit examination. |

^q1

> [!note] 按
> 「小考」是科举里考秀才之前的考试：县考、府考、道考（道是提学道，管一省学政的官），考过才得进县学成生员。这一条是野鹤自己的说明，[乾按]说「若以一卦而断数考必大谬」，是今人按语，不收。
>
> *Note:* The "minor examinations" are the examinations before the one that makes a licentiate: the county, prefecture and circuit examinations (the circuit being the education intendant who ran the schools of a province); passing them lets one enter the county school and become a student. This is the Old Man of Yehe's own explanation; the [Qian's note] saying that judging several examinations by one hexagram is a great mistake is a modern comment and is not quoted.
