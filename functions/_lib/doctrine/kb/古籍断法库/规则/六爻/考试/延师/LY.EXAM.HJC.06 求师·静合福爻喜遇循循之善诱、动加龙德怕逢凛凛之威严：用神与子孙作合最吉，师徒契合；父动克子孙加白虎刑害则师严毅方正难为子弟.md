# 求师·静合福爻喜遇循循之善诱、动加龙德怕逢凛凛之威严：用神与子孙作合最吉，师徒契合；父动克子孙加白虎刑害则师严毅方正难为子弟

六爻 · 考试 · 延师 · 出处 [[黄金策]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 用神静而和子孙爻相合最吉，老师博文约礼、循循善诱，师徒投契；怕父爻动而克子孙，再加白虎、刑、害，老师就严厉刚毅、凛然不可犯，子弟难受。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* The Subject still and combining with the Output line is most auspicious: the teacher is learned and restrained and draws one on step by step, and teacher and pupil are in accord; what is feared is the Resource line moving and controlling Output, with the White Tiger, punishment and harm besides, when the teacher is stern, resolute and so awesome that one dare not offend him, and the pupils fare hard.

## 《黄金策·求师》原文

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮正宗]] 第 1830 行 · Huangjin Ce (The Golden Strategy), "Seeking a teacher", base text
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 静合福爻， | （用神）静而合福爻， | If it is still and combines with the Output line, |
> | 喜遇循循之善诱； | 喜的是遇到循循善诱的老师； | one is glad to meet a teacher who draws one on step by step; |
> | 动加龙德， | 动而加上龙德， | if it moves and adds the Dragon Virtue, |
> | 怕逢凛凛之威严 | 怕碰到凛然的威严 | one fears meeting a stern and awe-inspiring dignity |

^q1

## 王洪绪注(《卜筮正宗》)

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮正宗]] 第 1830 行 · Wang Hongxu's commentary (Bushi Zhengzong)
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 用神与子孙作合最吉，必能博文约礼，循循善诱，甚得为师之道，必主师徒契合。 | 用神和子孙爻相合最吉，老师必定博文约礼、循循善诱，很得为师之道，主师徒契合。 | The Subject combining with the Output line is most auspicious: the teacher will surely be learned and restrained and draw one on step by step, has much of the way of a teacher, and teacher and pupil are in accord. |
> | 唯怕父动则克子孙，更加白虎刑害，必然难为子弟，主其师严毅方正，凛然而不可少犯。 | 只怕父爻动而克子孙，再加白虎、刑、害，必然让子弟难受，主这位老师严厉刚毅、方正，凛然不可稍有触犯。 | What is feared is the Resource line moving and controlling Output, with the White Tiger, punishment and harm besides: it surely makes things hard for the pupils, and means the teacher is stern, resolute and upright, so awesome that one dare not offend him in the least. |

^q2

> [!note] 按
> 「龙德」是神煞里的龙德星（书里没有在这里解释），「博文约礼」出自《论语》，「循循善诱」是一步一步引导。
>
> *Note:* The "Dragon Virtue" is the Dragon Virtue spirit-star (the book does not explain it here); "learned and restrained" comes from the Analects, and "draws one on step by step" is leading a pupil on gradually.
