# 僧官医官·亦喜官文发动（旧文谓僧道医官占功名以子孙为用神、不宜父动，作者以为非：他人占僧道医以子孙为用神，自占功名仍看官爻；官星持世或父母持世得旺官生，名必成；阴阳杂职嫌子动；书中例：屯之既济，子孙持世官临应，果退职）

六爻 · 事业 · 取象 · 出处 [[增删卜易]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 僧官、道纪、医官、杂职、阴阳等官：别人占僧人、道士、医生，以子孙为用神，子孙旺相则道业高强，父母兴旺则平庸愚笨、懦弱；他们自己占自己的道术，也以子孙为用神，子孙旺相能降伏鬼神。但他们自己占功名时，子孙发动就克官星了，不能再当用神，应当仍然看官爻：官星持世，或日月动爻来生，或父母持世、有旺相的官星来生，功名必定成；子孙发动或子孙持世，一定不成名；阴阳官和各种杂职官尤其嫌子孙发动；其余的和普通人求名同样断。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* Monastic, Daoist, medical, minor and yin-yang officials: when another person divines about a monk, Daoist or doctor, Output is the Subject, and if Output is thriving his practice is high and strong, if Resource flourishes he is mediocre, dull and timid; when they divine about their own art, Output is again the Subject, and a thriving Output can subdue ghosts and spirits. But when they divine about their own rank, a moving Output controls the star of office and can no longer be the Subject, so one must still look at the Pressure line: if the star of office holds the Self, or the Day, Month or a moving line generates it, or Resource holds the Self and a thriving star of office generates it, the rank will surely be made; if Output moves or holds the Self, the name will surely not be made; yin-yang officials and all minor posts especially dislike a moving Output; otherwise judge as for an ordinary man seeking a name.

> [!warning] 打架
> 黄金策仕宦章讲僧道医官「岂可文书发动」，旧注解作僧道医官的功名以子孙为用神、父母发动克子孙必有灾悔（LY.CAREER.HJC.27）；增删卜易引了这句旧文，说「余以为非也」，并且主张自占功名时看官爻、不看子孙。
>
> *Where they disagree:* The Huangjin Ce's chapter on holding office says of monastic, Daoist and medical officials 'how can the document move', and the old commentary explains it as: their rank takes Output as the Subject, and a moving Resource that controls Output surely brings misfortune and regret (LY.CAREER.HJC.27); the Zeng Shan Bu Yi quotes this old text and says 'I think this is wrong', holding that when they divine about their own rank one looks at the Pressure line and not at Output.

> [!note] 按
> 「僧官、道纪、医官、阴阳」是明清官署里管僧道、医、阴阳的官职；书里没有解释。「鬼服龙降」是说道术高强、能降伏鬼神。
>
> *Note:* Monastic officials, Daoist registrars, medical officials and yin-yang officials were the officers who administered monks, Daoists, medicine and divination in the Ming and Qing offices; the book does not explain them. 'Ghosts submit and dragons are tamed' says that the art is high and can subdue ghosts and spirits.
