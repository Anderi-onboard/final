# 断易通玄赋·禄马最为良（旧注：有禄马爻临世应，求官可进，名利俱成）

六爻 · 事业 · 贵人 · 出处 [[卜筮全书]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 有禄、有马的爻落在世爻或应爻上，求官可以进，名和利都能成。禄是日干的禄所在的地支（甲禄在寅、乙禄在卯），马是日支三合的驿马所在的地支（申子辰马在寅）。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* When a line that is the Emolument or the Horse stands on the Self or Other line, one seeking office can advance and both name and profit are achieved. The Emolument is the branch where the day-stem has its Emolument (Jia at Yin, Yi at Mao); the Horse is the branch of the Post Horse of the day's three-harmony group (Shen-Zi-Chen has its horse at Yin).

## 《卜筮全书·断易通玄赋》原文

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮大全]] 第 2961 行 · Bushi Quanshu (Complete Book of Divination), "The Duanyi Tongxuan Fu", base text
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 禄马最为良。 | 禄和马最好。 | The Emolument and the Horse are the best. |

^q1

## 旧注(《卜筮全书》所载,注者不详)

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮大全]] 第 2963–2964 行 · Old commentary (printed in the Bushi Quanshu; commentator unknown)
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 假如甲日有寅爻，则是禄也。 | 比如甲日，卦里有寅爻，这就是禄。 | For example, on a Jia day a Yin line is the Emolument. |
> | 即甲禄在寅，乙禄在卯。 | 就是说甲的禄在寅，乙的禄在卯。 | That is, Jia has its Emolument at Yin and Yi at Mao. |
> | 申子辰日有寅爻，则是马也。 | 申子辰日，卦里有寅爻，这就是马。 | On a Shen, Zi or Chen day a Yin line is the Horse. |
> | 即申子辰马居寅。 | 就是说申子辰的马在寅。 | That is, the horse of Shen-Zi-Chen is at Yin. |
> | 若有此爻临世应，求官可进，名利俱成。 | 如果有这样的爻临在世爻或应爻上，求官可以进，名和利都成。 | If such a line stands on the Self or Other line, one seeking office can advance, and both name and profit are achieved. |

^q2

> [!note] 按
> 「禄」是天元禄（日干在哪个地支有禄），「马」是驿马（三合局的第一位的冲位：申子辰马居寅）。书里说禄马全篇歌诀见后面的神杀章，就是卜筮大全末尾的神煞歌例（见 LY.CAREER.BSQS 神煞各条）。旧注只说求官，这两个神煞的一般用法见神煞歌例。「看六爻卦还是要以用神为主」是虎易评注，不收。
>
> *Note:* The "Emolument" is the Heavenly Emolument (the branch where the day-stem has its Emolument) and the "Horse" is the Post Horse (the branch that clashes the first of the three-harmony group: Shen-Zi-Chen has its horse at Yin). The book says the full verses on the Emolument and Horse are in the later chapter on spirit-stars, which is the Spirit-Star Verses at the end of the Bushi Daquan (see the spirit-star entries in this chapter). The old note speaks only of seeking office. The remark that the reading should still rest on the Subject is Hu Yi's commentary and is not quoted.
