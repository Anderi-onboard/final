# 官禄占·何官（一）：四直贵马聚身世更德合相扶在官印阳爻者宰辅，加刑刃大杀出将入相；太岁五爻生合世身朝仕，冲克外任；内卦动外任外卦动朝仕；阳宫阳爻文职阴宫阴爻武弁；世持岁破朝仕六部、外任布按都司；持月建巡方安抚，持日建郡邑之宰

六爻 · 事业 · 取象 · 出处 [[易隐]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 断是什么官：四直、贵人、驿马聚在身爻、世爻上，又有德神、合神相扶，落在官星、印星的阳爻上，必是宰相之位；再加刑、刃、大杀，是出将入相；再加太岁或五爻生合，必荣获赐宴。太岁与五爻生合世身，是在朝为官；冲克世身，是外任；世爻、官爻在内卦发动是外任，在外卦发动是朝官（五爻是君位，四爻六爻都挨近君位）。官星、贵人持世，在阳宫阳爻的是文职，在阴宫阴爻的是武官。世爻持岁破，在朝是六部等大衙门，外任是布政司、按察司、都司；持月建，是巡方、安抚、行人这类；持日建，是郡县的长官。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* To tell what office it is: if the four rulers, the Noble and the Horse gather on the Body and Self lines, and Virtue and combination also support them, and they sit on a yang line of the star of office and seal, it is surely the post of chief minister; with the punishing blade and great baleful spirits added, one goes out as a general and comes in as chancellor; with Tai Sui or the fifth line generating or combining, he is surely honoured with a gift of banquet. If Tai Sui and the fifth line generate or combine with the Self and Body, it is an official at court; if they clash with or control the Self and Body, a post outside; if the Self or the Pressure line moves in the inner trigram it is a post outside, and in the outer trigram a court official (the fifth is the ruler's seat, and the fourth and sixth are both near the ruler). If the star of office and the Noble hold the Self in a yang palace and on a yang line it is a civil post, and in a yin palace and on a yin line a military officer. If the Self holds the Year-break, at court it is in the great offices such as the Six Ministries, and outside it is in the provincial administration, judicial or military commissions; holding the Month it is an inspecting commissioner, a pacification commissioner or an envoy; holding the Day it is the head of a prefecture or county.

> [!note] 按
> 几处文字可疑，译文只按字面意思译：「后荫前卦也」文意不通；「六部通都在大也」应是指六部和通政、都察、大理这样的大衙门，译文只写成「六部和其他大衙门」；「风岁破取重臣」里的「风」字像是衍字。「阿衡」是商代伊尹的官名，后来指宰相；「鼎昧」应是「鼎鼐」（调和羹汤的鼎），比喻宰相调和国政。「布按都司」是明代省一级的布政使司、按察使司和都指挥使司。
>
> *Note:* Several places are doubtful and are translated only by the letter: 'the later shades the earlier hexagram' makes no sense; 'Six Ministries, Tong, Du, in the great' presumably points to the Six Ministries and great offices such as the Office of Transmission, the Censorate and the Court of Judicial Review, but is rendered only as 'the Six Ministries and the other great offices'; the character 'wind' in 'wind Year-break takes' looks superfluous. 'Aheng' is the title of Yi Yin of the Shang, later used for the chief minister, and 'dingmei' is presumably 'dingnai', the cauldron for blending broth, an image for the chancellor harmonizing the government. 'Bu-an-dusi' are the provincial administration, surveillance and military commissions of the Ming.
