# 朝廷占·爻象（鄱阳汪所性占例）：比屯豫利封建，涣萃利告庙，益随利郊天，升利封禅，晋宜受朝觐，屯豫利立君，豫既济师离谦利征伐，乾利即位，师解巽利田猎，比屯恒不利田猎，涣利发号施令，革利改朔，夬利去小人，师利赏战功，家人宜纳后，归妹宜嫁妹，蛊利求嗣，井利改邑，剥遁宜选宫嫔寺人，鼎利正位号，姤巽宜颁抚恤之诏，大有丰泰天下平，否国步艰，大过国祚危

六爻 · 事业 · 国朝 · 出处 [[易隐]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 什么卦利于做国家的什么事（鄱阳汪所性的占例）：比、屯、豫利于分封建国；涣、萃利于祭告宗庙；益、随利于郊祭天；升利于封禅；晋适宜接受朝觐；屯、豫利于立君；豫、既济、师、离、谦利于出兵征伐；乾利于即位；师、解、巽利于田猎，比、屯、恒不利于田猎；涣利于发号施令；革利于改定历法；夬利于除去小人；师利于赏战功；家人适宜纳后；归妹适宜嫁妹；蛊利于求嗣；井利于迁改城邑；剥、遁适宜选宫嫔、宦官；鼎利于确定名位称号；姤、巽适宜颁布抚恤的诏书；大有、丰、泰，天下太平；否，国运艰难；大过，国祚危险。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* Which hexagram favours which act of state (the divination examples of Wang Suoxing of Poyang). Bi, Zhun and Yu favour enfeoffment; Huan and Cui favour reporting to the ancestral temple; Yi and Sui favour the suburban sacrifice to Heaven; Sheng favours the Feng and Shan sacrifices; Jin is fit for receiving court audiences; Zhun and Yu favour setting up a ruler; Yu, Jiji, Shi, Li and Qian (Modesty) favour military campaigns; Qian (Heaven) favours accession; Shi, Xie and Xun favour hunting, while Bi, Zhun and Heng are unfavourable for it; Huan favours issuing proclamations and orders; Ge favours changing the calendar; Guai favours removing petty men; Shi favours rewarding military merit; Jiaren is fit for taking an empress; Guimei is fit for marrying off a sister; Gu favours seeking an heir; Jing favours moving a city; Bo and Dun are fit for selecting palace consorts and eunuchs; Ding favours settling titles and ranks; Gou and Xun are fit for issuing edicts of relief; Dayou, Feng and Tai mean the world at peace; Pi means the state's course is hard; Daguo means the dynasty is in danger.

> [!note] 按
> 这一段是《易隐》引的鄱阳汪所性的「占例」，是一张「哪一卦适宜办哪一件国事」的清单，没有给理由。「谦」读 qian，与乾同音，译文写成 Qian （Modesty） 和 Qian （Heaven） 来分。「封禅」是帝王到泰山祭天地，「改朔」是改定历法（换正月），「嫁妹」按字面是出嫁妹妹，「抚恤之诏」是抚慰救济灾民的诏书，「国步」是国运，「国祚」是国运的年数。「朝观」原文作「朝观」，按文意是朝觐。
>
> *Note:* This passage is the 'divination examples' of Wang Suoxing of Poyang as quoted in the Yi Yin, a list of which hexagram is fit for which affair of state, giving no reasons. 'Qian', modesty, is pronounced as Qian, heaven, and the translation writes Qian (Modesty) and Qian (Heaven) to tell them apart. 'Fengshan' is the emperor's sacrifice to Heaven and Earth at Mount Tai, 'gai shuo' is fixing a new calendar (changing the first month), 'jia mei' is by the letter marrying off a younger sister, 'the edict of relief' is an edict comforting and relieving disaster victims, 'guobu' is the course of the state, and 'guozuo' is the span of the dynasty. The text writes 'chaoguan' where by sense it is the court audience.
