# 月将值日·功曹（月建寅）占病：白虎神、寒热头痛、胸肋疼闷气冲心、手足拘攀行不得；触犯山司木下神；寅宫卦断伤亡五道东北土煞怪巽入宅、禳度即安轻

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[断易鬼灵经]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 月建落在寅、叫功曹的时候占病：功曹当值，是白虎神，要赶紧祭祀超度，免得有哭声；病人忽冷忽热，头疼，胸肋疼闷，气往上冲心，手脚拘挛，走不动。冲犯的是山司和木下神。「寅宫卦断」那一段说：还有伤亡鬼和五道，东北方的土煞又加上来，怪巽进了宅作祸；只要做禳解超度，马上安稳减轻。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* When the Month falls on Yin, named Gongcao, divining illness: when Gongcao rules the day it is the White Tiger spirit, and offerings and deliverance should be made at once, to avoid the sound of weeping; the sick person has chills and fever, a headache, the chest and ribs aching and oppressed with breath surging against the heart, and the hands and feet drawn and cramped so that he cannot walk. What has been offended is the Mountain Official and the spirit under the wood. The passage 'the verdict for the Yin-palace hexagrams' says: there are also the dead by injury and the Five Ways, the Earth Killer of the northeast comes on top, and the uncanny Xun has come into the house to make trouble; if rites of averting and deliverance are performed, he is soon at ease and lighter.

> [!note] 按
> 格式和名词见 LY.ILL.GLJ.01。这一首的头一句「功曹值日白虎神」是十二首里唯一直接点出六神的，把功曹和白虎神连在一起，书里没有说为什么。「拘攀」读作拘挛；「怪巽」书里没有解释，「巽」是八卦里的巽（东南），像是说东南方来的怪，是读法；最后一句「世须禳度」的「世」字不通，像是「但」或「只」的误写，译文按「只要」读，这是读法；「安轻」是安稳、减轻。
>
> *Note:* For the form and the terms see LY.ILL.GLJ.01. The first line of this verse, 'when Gongcao rules the day, there is the White Tiger spirit', is the only one among the twelve that names one of the six spirits directly, tying Gongcao to the White Tiger, and the book does not say why. 'Drawn and cramped' reads the book's word for being drawn and clutching as 'cramped'; the book does not explain 'the uncanny Xun', where 'Xun' is the Xun of the eight trigrams (southeast), and it looks like an uncanny thing coming from the southeast, which is a reading; in the last line the first character of 'if rites of averting and deliverance are performed' does not make sense as printed and looks like a miswriting of 'only' or 'but', and the translation reads it as 'if', which is a reading; 'at ease and lighter' is 'settled and eased'.
