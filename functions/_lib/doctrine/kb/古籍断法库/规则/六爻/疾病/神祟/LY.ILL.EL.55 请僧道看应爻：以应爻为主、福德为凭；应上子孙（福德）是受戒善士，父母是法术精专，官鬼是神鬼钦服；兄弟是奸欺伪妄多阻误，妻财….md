# 请僧道看应爻：以应爻为主、福德为凭；应上子孙（福德）是受戒善士，父母是法术精专，官鬼是神鬼钦服；兄弟是奸欺伪妄多阻误，妻财是贪利好色、非通天之流；子孙临白虎是恶辈，福德带青龙是善人

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[易林补遗]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 请僧接道是为了招请贤者，要看的是应爻上站着什么。以应爻为主，以福德（子孙）为凭。应爻上是子孙（福德），这个僧道确实是受了戒、有行持的善人；父母临着应，法术精深专一；官鬼临着应，神鬼都敬服；兄弟临着应，多奸诈虚伪、常常阻误；妻财临着应，是贪婪图利、好色营财的人，不是能通天彻地的人。子孙临白虎，是恶人，心里容不下凶险；福德带青龙，是善人，有慈善，心里常存德行。占卦只看应爻，就知道休咎。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* To invite monks and Daoists in order to summon the worthy, look at what stands on the Other line. The Other line is the main line, and Output (the fortune spirit) is the ground. Output on the Other line: he is a good man who has received the precepts and has real conduct; Resource on it: his rites and arts are refined and exclusive; Pressure on it: gods and ghosts admire and submit; Peer on it: crafty, deceitful and false, and often blocking and erring; Wealth on it: a man greedy for profit, lustful and scheming for wealth, not one of those who penetrate heaven and earth. Output on the White Tiger is a wicked man; Output (the fortune spirit) with the Azure Dragon is a good man, compassionate, whose heart always keeps virtue. In divining, look only at the Other line, and one knows good and ill.

> [!note] 按
> 这一章说的是请僧道做法事时，怎么看请来的这位僧道是贤是愚。章首小标是「以应爻为主，福德为凭」：占的是「所请的僧道」这个人，他站在应爻上，所以看应爻和它上面的六亲、六神。和这本书别的章一样，先是歌诀、再是解说；这里的解说文字多处含混，译文里标出。「职悔业根」按上下文读作「忏悔业根」；「慈惠降龙」「有法灵伏虎」用的是僧道降龙伏虎的典故，只按字面译；「以亲污积」不成句；「乃鄙入蝎荤」按「乃鄙人〔嗜〕荤」的大意译，并标含混；「盗助」「欺人机有慈善」都不成句，译文里保留并标出。「福德在时」的福德就是子孙，所以「子孙乃僧道也」和「福德即子孙也」是一个意思：子孙在这里既是福德、又是僧道本人。 这一段里神、经、法事的名字，书里没有解释，这里按字面译或音译，不作认定。
>
> *Note:* This chapter is about how to tell, when inviting monks and Daoists to perform rites, whether the one invited is worthy or foolish. The chapter's subtitle is 'the Other line is the main line, and Output is the ground': what is divined is the monk or Daoist invited, who stands on the Other line, so one looks at the Other line and the six relations and six spirits on it. As in the book's other chapters, verse comes first and then the commentary; the commentary here is loose in many places, and the translation marks them. 'Zhi hui ye gen' is read as 'repenting the roots of karma' from the context; 'the compassionate ones who subdue dragons' and 'the efficacious ones who subdue tigers' use the tale of monks and Daoists subduing dragons and tigers, and are translated only literally; 'yi qin wu ji' is not a sentence; 'nai bi ru xie hun' is translated in the general sense of 'a base man craving meat', and marked as garbled; 'dao zhu' and 'qi ren ji you ci shan' are not sentences, and the translation keeps them and marks them. The 'fortune spirit' in 'when the fortune spirit is present' is Output, so 'Output is the monk or Daoist' and 'the fortune spirit is Output' mean the same thing: Output here is both the fortune spirit and the monk or Daoist himself. Names of gods, spirits, scriptures and rites in this passage are given by transliteration or plain rendering; the book does not explain them, and none is identified here beyond what the book says.
