# 按八宫、五行、六神推病因：乾兑鬼兴祸因寺庙、艮坤官发患系丘陵、坎离南北、震巽园林；木鬼砍伐、金鬼刀兵、土鬼开辟、水江河、火城市；武酒色风寒、龙讴吟歌舞、雀斗争气怒、虎哭泣哀丧、蛇惊、勾跌——注举四例，戒毋执一，唯理之当

六爻 · 疾病 · 病因 · 出处 [[易冒]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 所以乾、兑两宫鬼旺，祸因出在寺庙；艮、坤两宫官鬼发动，忧患牵涉丘陵；坎、离说的是南北，震、巽说的是园林。木鬼是砍伐惹的，金鬼是刀兵的惊恐，土鬼是开挖动土的妨害，水是江河，火是城市。玄武是因为酒色风寒，青龙是唱歌吟咏歌舞，朱雀是争斗生气，白虎是哭泣丧事，螣蛇是从惊吓来的，勾陈是从跌倒起的。注举了四个例子：同一天占的几卦，乾宫发动的是被马撞伤，坎宫玄武是捕鱼得病，火鬼带螣蛇是在客馆听到失火吓出来的，巳午两鬼带青龙咸池是酒色所致——都不是按乾兑寺庙、坎为江河、火鬼城市、震巽园林去断，所以总的告诫是：不要死抠一条，只看合不合理，以及人心的灵巧善于推求。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* So when the ghost thrives in Qian or Dui, the misfortune comes from temples and shrines; when Pressure moves in Gen or Kun, the trouble is tied to mounds and hills; Kan and Li mean south and north, Zhen and Xun mean gardens and groves. A Wood ghost comes from felling trees, a Metal ghost from the fear of blades and soldiers, an Earth ghost from the hindrance of breaking ground; Water is rivers, and Fire is towns. The Dark Tortoise comes from wine, lust and wind-cold; the Azure Dragon is singing, chanting and dance; the Vermilion Bird is quarrels and temper; the White Tiger is weeping and mourning; the Coiling Snake comes from fright, and Hook Earth arises from a fall. The note gives four cases, all divined on the same day: a Qian-palace mover meant being trampled by a horse, a Dark Tortoise ghost in Kan meant catching fish, a Fire ghost with the Coiling Snake meant a fright at hearing of a fire at an inn, and Si and Wu ghosts with the Azure Dragon and Xianchi meant wine and lust; none was judged by temples for Qian and Dui, rivers for Kan, towns for a Fire ghost or gardens for Zhen and Xun, and so the closing warning: do not cling to one rule; go only by what reason fits, and by the mind's quick skill at working things out.

> [!note] 按
> 经文这几句在黄金策鬼神章里也有对应的配法（八宫见 LY.ILL.HJC.95，六神见 HJC.96，五行见 HJC.97），不同的是易冒把它们排成一串列在「病从何起」之后，只作起病之由，不作鬼神名目；而且后面的注立刻给四个例子说明「不要拘泥」。「坎离南北」的次序原文如此（坎在北、离在南，这里颠倒了），没有再解释。注里第二例说的「乾为僧」是乾配僧道，「午官为马」是午配马，都取自八卦、地支的象；这四个例子放在一起，是为了证明同一个配法在不同的卦里读出来的东西可以完全不同。
>
> *Note:* The assignments in these lines have parallels in the Huangjin Ce's chapter on ghosts and spirits (the palaces at LY.ILL.HJC.95, the six spirits at HJC.96, the phases at HJC.97); the difference is that the Yi Mao sets them in a run after 'where the illness rose from', as causes of falling ill and not as names of ghosts and spirits, and that the note immediately gives four cases to show 'do not be rigid'. The order 'Kan and Li, south and north' is as in the original (Kan is in fact north and Li south, so the two lists run in opposite order) and is not explained. In the note's first case 'Qian is monks' assigns monks and Daoists to Qian, and 'the Wu Pressure line is the horse' assigns the horse to Wu, both taken from the images of the trigrams and branches; the four cases are set together to show that the same assignments read from different hexagrams can give quite different things.
