# 入门来听断·来问时门前所见：慌忙来问官司病者在床中；入门见老男儿病者安然无伤损；孩儿哭啼病患都皆有；入门一灯病者见凶星；三人话病者最是鸟鸡子

六爻 · 疾病 · 取象 · 出处 [[断易鬼灵经]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 进门的时候看到的人和物、听到的话，先断一个大概，讲到病的几句是：慌慌张张来问事，必凶，病的人躺在床上；进门先见一个老年男子，病人平安，没有损伤，像枯木碰上了春天；进门看见小孩哭哭啼啼，家里不吉利，破财、病患都有；进门见遇三个人说话，官司里官鬼发动，病人最要紧的是（见到）鸟、鸡、子之类，阎王一定要请他回去；进门见一盏灯，病人见凶星，要斋神求福，保祷度过就不相牵连；进门一炷香，家宅大吉大昌，官司马上消散，病人得以离床。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* What is seen and heard on entering the door gives a first rough judgment, and the lines that speak of illness are these: if he comes in a flurry to ask, it is surely ominous, and the sick are in bed; if an old man is seen first on entering, the sick one is safe and unharmed, like a withered tree meeting spring; if a child is seen crying on entering, the household is unlucky, and loss of money and illness are all present; if on entering he meets three people talking, then in an official matter Pressure moves, and the sick one's chief matter is (the sight of) a bird, a hen or a child, and Yama will surely call him back; if on entering he sees a lamp, the sick one meets an ominous star, and he should keep the fast to the spirits and ask for blessing, and with prayer he passes through without being dragged in; if on entering there is a stick of incense, the household is greatly fortunate and prosperous, the official matter dissolves at once, and the sick one gets up from his bed.

> [!note] 按
> 这是附录里的一首长诗，没有标点，一口气念下来，按「入门见某物」一条一条断：官司、失物、婚姻、买卖、病各条里都有几句，这里只收讲到病的六条（其余：「衣冠来问必婚姻」「入门见一鸡鸭随」「入门见宁纱」「入门见柴木」四条没有讲病，不收）。这一首的字句多处不通，译文是最接近字面的读法，不当作确切的意思：「入门是大正无悠忧」的「大正」「悠忧」，「病是凶神为福守」，「病者最是鸟鸡子」（鸟鸡子是鸟、鸡、子，还是「乌鸡子」，不能定），「齐神」（像是「斋神」），「病者得难床」（「难」疑是「离」之误，读作离床，是读法）都是这样。「病者最是鸟鸡子 阎王必要请回归」是死兆，「病者安然无伤损 如喜枯木正逢春」是吉兆；「入门见一灯」那条里「病者见凶星」后面接的是「齐神求为福 保度不相牵」，是说见凶星要去求神保祷。这里写的「入门」是问卦的人走进门来，还是医者进病家的门，书里没有说，译文写作「进门」，不定主语。
>
> *Note:* This is a long poem in the appendix, without punctuation, read off in one breath, judging item by item from 'on entering the door he sees such-and-such': each item has a few lines on a lawsuit, lost goods, marriage, trade and illness, and only the six items that speak of illness are taken here (four others, 'one who comes in robes and cap to ask is surely about marriage', 'on entering he sees a hen and a duck following', 'on entering he sees plain gauze' and 'on entering he sees firewood', do not speak of illness and are not taken). The wording of this poem does not make sense in many places, and the translation is the reading closest to the letter and is not given as the sure sense: 'greatly upright' and 'no worry' in 'on entering it is greatly upright and there is no worry', 'illness: an ominous spirit that keeps guard for blessing', 'the sick one's chief matter is a bird, a hen or a child' (whether it is a bird, a hen and a child or a 'black hen's chick' cannot be settled), 'keep the fast to the spirits' (it looks like the fast to the spirits) and 'the sick one gets up from his bed' (the character printed as 'hard' looks like a misprint of 'leave', and is read as getting up, which is a reading) are all like this. 'The sick one's chief matter is a bird, a hen or a child; Yama will surely call him back' is a sign of death, and 'the sick one is safe and unharmed, like a withered tree meeting spring' a sign of good; in the item 'on entering he sees a lamp', after 'the sick one meets an ominous star' come 'keep the fast to the spirits and ask for blessing, with prayer he passes through and is not dragged in', which says that on meeting an ominous star he should ask the spirits for protection. The book does not say whether the 'entering' is the asker coming in at the door or a healer entering the sick person's house, and the translation writes 'on entering' without fixing the subject.
