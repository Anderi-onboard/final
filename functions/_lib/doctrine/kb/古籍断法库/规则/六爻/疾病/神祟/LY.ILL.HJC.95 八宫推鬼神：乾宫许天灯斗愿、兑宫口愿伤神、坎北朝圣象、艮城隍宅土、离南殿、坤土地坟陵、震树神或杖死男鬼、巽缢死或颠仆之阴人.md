# 八宫推鬼神：乾宫许天灯斗愿、兑宫口愿伤神、坎北朝圣象、艮城隍宅土、离南殿、坤土地坟陵、震树神或杖死男鬼、巽缢死或颠仆之阴人

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[黄金策]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 按鬼所在的卦宫推：在乾宫，是许过点天灯、拜北斗的愿；在兑宫，是口头许的愿或者伤神；在坎宫，是北方朝拜的圣像；在艮宫，是城隍或宅里的土神；在离宫，是南边的殿宇；在坤宫，是土地神或坟墓陵园；在震宫，是树神，或者被杖打死的男鬼；在巽宫，一定是吊死的，或者癫狂跌倒而死的女人。把八宫仔细推敲，各路鬼自然会显应出来。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* Work it out by the palace in which the ghost lies. In the Qian palace there was a vow of a heavenly lamp or to the Dipper; in the Dui palace, a spoken vow or an injuring spirit; in Kan, the sacred image worshipped in the north; in Gen, the City God or the earth spirit of the house; in Li, the southern hall; in Kun, the Earth God or graves and tombs; in Zhen, a tree spirit or a male ghost beaten to death with a staff; in Xun, surely one who hanged himself, or a woman who fell in a fit. Work out the eight palaces carefully and the various ghosts will make themselves apparent.

## 王洪绪注(《卜筮正宗》)

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮正宗]] 第 3375 行 · Wang Hongxu's commentary (Bushi Zhengzong)
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 此以八卦推之，乾象为天神，在此宫属火，宜许点天灯斗愿类。 | 这是按八卦来推：乾的象是天神，鬼在这个宫又属火，该是许过点天灯、拜北斗之类的愿。 | This is worked out by the eight trigrams: Qian is the image of the heavenly spirit, and a ghost in this palace, being Fire, should mean a vow such as lighting a heavenly lamp or to the Dipper. |

^q3

> [!note] 按
> 王洪绪引经文时，把「震恐树神，或杖伤之男鬼」写成「震恐神煞杖伤之男鬼」，把「八宫」写成「八卦」，「颠仆」写成「癫仆」的另一写法；他自己的注只讲了乾宫一例，其余各宫的推法只见于旧注。旧注里的「木鬼许放风筝」「子丑二爻许拜北斗」经文里没有，是旧注自己添的。「呪咀」是咒骂诅咒；「囹圄」「癫」的解释见卜筮大全篇末生僻字注。旧注最后一段解释了为什么以兑而不以乾为伤神：乾三画连是完整的金，兑上缺是有损伤的金。
>
> *Note:* When Wang Hongxu quotes the verse he writes 'Zhen may well be a spirit-star beaten with a staff' where the Bushi Daquan has 'a tree spirit, or a male ghost beaten to death', and 'the eight trigrams' for 'the eight palaces'; his own commentary treats only the Qian example, and the workings for the other palaces are found only in the old commentary. 'A Wood ghost means a vow to fly a kite' and 'on the lines of Zi and Chou, a vow to the Dipper' are not in the verse and are the old commentary's own additions. 'Zhouzu' means cursing and imprecation. The last passage of the old commentary explains why Dui and not Qian is the injuring spirit: Qian's three unbroken lines are whole Metal, and Dui, open at the top, is damaged Metal.
