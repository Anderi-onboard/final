# 离宫鬼化水是痰火、乾宫鬼化木是头风；震鬼遇螣蛇发动是惊悸颠狂、逢冲有逾墙上屋之患；艮鬼逢火鬼是痈疽，变出土鬼是浮肿蛊胀

六爻 · 疾病 · 症候 · 出处 [[黄金策]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 离宫的鬼变成水（或水爻动变成鬼）是痰火的症候；乾宫的鬼变成木（或木爻动变成鬼）是头风、眩晕。震宫的鬼在外卦，不要按脚上的病断，可以说他坐卧不安、心神恍惚（震是主动的卦）；再加螣蛇发动，是癫狂、惊痫，小孩是惊风；遇冲有翻墙上屋的乱动之患。艮宫的鬼遇上火鬼，一定生痈疽；变出土鬼，可以说浮肿、蛊胀一类。其余照此类推。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* A Pressure line of the Li palace changing into Water (or a moving Water line changing into Pressure) is the syndrome of phlegm-fire; a Pressure line of the Qian palace changing into Wood (or a moving Wood line changing into Pressure) is headwind and dizziness. A Zhen Pressure line in the outer trigram is not judged as a foot illness: say the patient cannot sit or lie at ease and his mind wanders (Zhen governs movement); with the Coiling Snake added and moving it is mania and fright-epilepsy, in a child convulsions; meeting a clash there is the danger of climbing over walls and onto roofs. A Gen Pressure line meeting a Fire Pressure surely brings carbuncles; if an Earth Pressure changes out, one can speak of swelling and drum-like distension. The rest follows by analogy.

> [!note] 按
> 「痰火」是痰和火郁在一起的病，「头风」是反复发作的头痛，「蛊胀」是肚腹胀大如鼓的病，「踰墙上屋」是发狂时翻墙爬房的举动。旧注「震在外卦，勿依此断」的「此」没有写明，王洪绪写成「勿以脚断」，译文按他的写法理解。旧注「艮象为山，山高故也」放在「逢冲」和「更逢火鬼」之间，没有说它解释的是哪一句（前面的翻墙上屋，还是后面的痈疽）；王洪绪把这句删了。经文的「巳午」是火的两个地支，「交重」就是发动。
>
> *Note:* 'Phlegm-fire' is an illness in which phlegm and fire are bound together; 'headwind' is a headache that comes back again and again; 'gu distension' is a belly swollen like a drum; 'climbing over walls and onto roofs' is what a patient in a fit of madness does. The 'this' of the old commentary's 'if Zhen is in the outer trigram, do not judge by this' is not spelled out, and Wang Hongxu writes 'do not judge it as a foot illness'; the translation follows his wording. The old commentary puts 'the image of Gen is the mountain, because a mountain is high' between 'meeting a clash' and 'meeting a Fire Pressure as well' and does not say which of the two it explains (the climbing of walls and roofs before it, or the carbuncles after it); Wang Hongxu drops the sentence. Si and Wu in the verse are the two Fire branches, and 'jiaozhong' simply means moving.
