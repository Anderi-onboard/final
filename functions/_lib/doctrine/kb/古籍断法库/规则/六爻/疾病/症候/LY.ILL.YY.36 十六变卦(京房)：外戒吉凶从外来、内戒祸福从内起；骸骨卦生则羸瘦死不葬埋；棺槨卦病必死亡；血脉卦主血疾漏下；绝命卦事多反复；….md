# 十六变卦(京房)：外戒吉凶从外来、内戒祸福从内起；骸骨卦生则羸瘦死不葬埋；棺槨卦病必死亡；血脉卦主血疾漏下；绝命卦事多反复；游魂肌肉卦精神恍惚；归魂塚墓卦坟墓吉而事可成

六爻 · 疾病 · 症候 · 出处 [[易隐]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 每一宫都有十六个变卦，占的人遇到变入本宫卦的，灾福应验十分。外戒卦，吉凶从外面来；内戒卦，祸福从里面起；骸骨卦，活着的人羸弱消瘦，死了的人不得埋葬；棺槨卦，病人必定死亡；血脉卦，主血疾和漏下；绝命卦，事情多反复，为人孤独、和世俗不合；游魂、肌肉卦，精神恍惚，像做梦、像痴呆；归魂、坟墓卦，坟墓吉，事可以成。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* Each palace has sixteen changed hexagrams, and when the diviner meets a change into a hexagram of the own palace, calamity and fortune are borne out to the full. An Outer Warning hexagram: good and ill come from outside; an Inner Warning hexagram: calamity and fortune arise from within; a Bones hexagram: the living are frail and thin, the dead are not buried; a Coffin hexagram: in illness the person surely dies; a Blood Vessels hexagram means blood disease and leakage from below; an End of Life hexagram: affairs mostly reverse and repeat, and the person is solitary and out of tune with the customs; a Wandering Soul or Flesh hexagram: the spirit is dazed, as in a dream or as if foolish; a Returning Soul or Grave hexagram: graves are auspicious and the matter can succeed.

> [!note] 按
> 这是京房的「十六变卦」：每一宫从本宫卦起，一爻一爻地变（「飞」就是变一爻），依次得到十六个卦，其中几个有专名（游魂、外戒、内戒、归魂、绝命、血脉、肌肉、骸骨、棺槨、坟墓）。头一段是乾宫的走法：乾变姤、遁、否、观、剥（五变），上爻不动；再从剥变第四爻得晋（游魂），变第三爻得旅（外戒），变第二爻得鼎（内戒），变初爻得大有（归魂）；再从大有变第二爻得离（绝命），变第三爻得噬嗑（血脉），变第四爻得颐（肌肉），变第五爻得益（骸骨），再变第四爻得无妄（棺槨），变第三爻得同人（坟墓），变第二爻回到乾。「自初至五不动」按「从初到五逐爻变、上爻不动」读，因为下文说「上爻为宗庙，永不变」。后一段是这些名称的断语，只有「棺槨卦，病必死亡」和「骸骨卦」「血脉卦」直接说到病；「游魂肌肉卦精神恍惚」和卜筮元龟的「游魂恍惚」是同一个说法。「塚墓」即冢墓，按 Grave 译。「遇变入本宫卦者」的「本宫卦」是本卦所属那一宫里的卦。断易天机里的卜筮元龟一段用了这些名称，见 LY.ILL.DYTJ.13。
>
> *Note:* This is Jing Fang's 'sixteen changed hexagrams': each palace, starting from the palace hexagram, changes one line at a time ('fei', flying, means changing one line) and gives sixteen hexagrams in turn, several of which have their own names (Wandering Soul, Outer Warning, Inner Warning, Returning Soul, End of Life, Blood Vessels, Flesh, Bones, Coffin, Grave). The first passage gives the walk for the Qian palace: Qian changes to Gou, Dun, Pi, Guan and Bo (five changes), the top line not moving; then from Bo changing the fourth line gives Jin (Wandering Soul), the third line Lü (Outer Warning), the second line Ding (Inner Warning), the first line Dayou (Returning Soul); then from Dayou changing the second line gives Li (End of Life), the third line Shihe (Blood Vessels), the fourth line Yi (Nourishment; Flesh), the fifth line Yi (Increase; Bones), then the fourth line again gives Wuwang (Coffin), the third line Tongren (Grave), and the second line returns to Qian. 'Zi chu zhi wu bu dong' is read as 'changing line by line from the first to the fifth, the top line not moving', because the text goes on to say 'the top line is the ancestral temple and never changes'. The second passage gives the judgments for these names, and only 'a Coffin hexagram: in illness the person surely dies', and the Bones and Blood Vessels hexagrams, speak of illness directly; 'a Wandering Soul or Flesh hexagram: the spirit is dazed' is the same saying as the 'Wandering Soul dazed' of the Bushi Yuangui. 'Zhong mu' (a tomb mound) is rendered Grave. The 'hexagram of the own palace' of 'a change into a hexagram of the own palace' is a hexagram in the palace to which the original hexagram belongs. The passage of the Bushi Yuangui in the Duanyi Tianji uses these names, see LY.ILL.DYTJ.13.
