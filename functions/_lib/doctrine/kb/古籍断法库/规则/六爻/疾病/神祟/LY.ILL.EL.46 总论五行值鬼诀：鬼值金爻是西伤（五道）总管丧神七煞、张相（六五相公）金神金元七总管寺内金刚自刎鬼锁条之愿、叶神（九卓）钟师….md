# 总论五行值鬼诀：鬼值金爻是西伤（五道）总管丧神七煞、张相（六五相公）金神金元七总管寺内金刚自刎鬼锁条之愿、叶神（九卓）钟师（钟将军）武安王（关公）；木爻是茶延枷锁船神草野家堂萧公（五圣）东岳树神山神九良星煞悬梁（缢死）鬼；水爻是观音三官北斗龙王祠山大帝金龙四大王水仙杜氏河太曹三施宋二相晏公井泉童子徐大将军河伯水官溺死魂；火爻是赵玄坛五福五显灵官火德星君三煞天灯香愿南堂五圣灶神焚烧鬼；土爻是城隍社庙神五方贤圣土皇土地太岁螣蛇皮肠大王瘟疫鬼；此论五行所属，仍须把六兽入官细看、轻重同看，不可胡判

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 官鬼落在哪一行的爻上，就是哪一路的神鬼。金爻：是煞，还有西伤（就是五道）；总管、丧神、七煞；张相（就是六五相公）和金神七煞、金元七总管、寺里的金刚、自刎的鬼、锁条的愿；叶神（就是九卓）、钟师（就是钟将军）、武安王（就是关公）。木爻：茶延、枷锁、船神、草野、家堂、萧公（就是五圣）、东岳、树神、山神、九良星煞，和悬梁的鬼（就是上吊死的鬼）。水爻：观音、三官、北斗、龙王、祠山大帝、金龙四大王、水仙、杜氏夫人、河太、曹三、施相公、宋相公、晏公、坑厕的主、井泉童子、徐大将军、河伯水官，和溺死的魂。火爻：赵玄坛、五福大神、五显灵官、火德星君、三煞、天灯香愿、南堂五圣、灶神，和被火烧死的鬼。土爻：城隍、社庙里的神、五方贤圣、土皇、土地、太岁、螣蛇、皮肠大王，和瘟疫鬼。这里只是论五行各属哪些神，还要再把六兽放进官鬼里去寻，轻重也照这样比较，千万不要随口乱判是哪一位鬼神。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* The Pressure line on a line of a given phase gives that kind of god or ghost. A Metal line: a killing star, and further the Western Injury (the Five Roads); the Chief Official, the mourning spirits, the Seven Killings; Lord Zhang (Lord Liuwu) with the Golden Spirit and the Seven Killings, the Chief Official Jin Yuanqi, the vajra guardians in temples, ghosts who cut their own throats, and the vow of the chain; the Leaf God (Jiuzhuo), Master Zhong (General Zhong) and the King of Martial Peace (Guan Gong). A Wood line: the Tea Feast, the cangue and fetters, the boat god, the wilds, the household shrine, Lord Xiao (the Five Sages), the Eastern Peak, the tree god, the Mountain God and the Nine Good Stars, and the ghost of hanging from a beam (that is, one who hanged himself). A Water line: Guanyin, the Three Officials, the Northern Dipper, the Dragon King, the Great Emperor of Ci Mountain, the Golden Dragon Fourth Great King, the Water Immortal, Lady Du, He Tai, Cao San, Minister Shi, Minister Song, Lord Yan, the master of pit and privy, the youth of the well and spring, General Xu, the River Count and the water officials, and the souls of the drowned. A Fire line: Zhao Xuantan, the Great God of the Five Blessings, the Five Manifest Spirit Officials, the Fire Virtue Star Lord, the Three Killings, the heaven lamp and incense vow, the Five Sages of the Southern Hall, the Kitchen God, and ghosts burned to death. An Earth line: the City God, the gods of shrines and temples, the sages of the five quarters, the Earth Emperor, the Earth God, Tai Sui, the Coiling Snake, the Great King Pichang, and ghosts of epidemic. This only discusses which gods belong to which phase; one must further bring the six beasts into the Pressure line to search, weigh the light and the heavy in the same way, and on no account judge which ghost or god it is by idle talk.

> *Note:* This is the second section of the 'general discourse' (numbered 'two'): each phase the Pressure line falls in is matched with a string of divine names. Earlier in this chapter (LY.ILL.EL.30 to EL.34) the phases were matched with the six beasts one by one, and here the names are compressed into a quick-reference table; the last sentence, 'then bring the six beasts into the Pressure line to search', points back there: first look at the phase, then at the beast, and judge only with the two together, never from one alone. Doubts about a few names: 'Jingang' (with an unusual second character) is read as the vajra guardian from the 'vajra guardians in temples' in the commentary below and is transliterated; 'cha ting' and the commentary's 'cha yan' are the same, and are handled as 'Tea Feast' (as translated in LY.ILL.EL.39); 'Xingwu' is probably 'Zhenwu' (the Yi Yin's Water ghost list is 'Guanyin, Zhenwu, the Three Officials', LY.ILL.YY.28, in the same order as here), and is transliterated as written; the 'tang' in 'He Tai Cao Tang' does not agree with the commentary's 'Cao San' and both are translated as written; the 'yi di' in 'the Three Officials and the Emperor of the Post' is hard to understand and may be a slip for 'the Three Officials the Great Emperors', and is translated literally; 'earth-husk spirits' is probably 'earth-and-grain spirits' (the Yi Yin's Earth ghost list reads 'the City God, the Earth-and-Grain, Tai Sui, the Great King Pichang', LY.ILL.YY.28), and the Yi Yin writes 'Pichang' with a different character for 'chang'; all are translated as written; the 'sha' in 'a ghost on a Metal line is a killing star' is the general term for a killing air or ill-omened star. 'Yue si' is as in the previous entries, read as drowned. The correspondences 'Five Roads', 'Jiuzhuo', 'General Zhong', 'Guan Gong' and 'Lord Liuwu' are given by the commentary itself and are copied here, without further identification. Names of gods, spirits and stars in this passage are given by transliteration or plain rendering; they are cults and stars of the Wu region that the book does not explain, and none is identified here beyond what the book says.
