# 合处逢冲·占病：惟有占讼与病喜遇合处逢冲；合则事必成、逢冲灾必散、事将危而有救病欲死而复生；吉神合处不可冲、凶神合处喜逢冲

六爻 · 疾病 · 生死 · 出处 [[断易天机]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 合处逢冲：比如占得雷地豫卦，世应相生，六爻相合，吉，没有不利的，是事事可以成的样子；却是在子日占得，子冲应上的午、害世上的未，这就是合处逢冲。只有占官司和占病，喜欢遇到合处逢冲：合，事就一定成；逢冲，灾就一定散；事将危而有救，病欲死而复生。其余照这样类推。又说：吉神合的地方不可以冲，凶神合的地方喜欢逢冲。大概可以照这个断。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* A clash where there is a combination: for example, divining the hexagram Thunder over Earth, Yu: the Self and the Other generate each other, the six lines combine, lucky and nothing unfavourable, the image of every matter succeeding; but it is divined on a Zi day, and Zi clashes with the Wu on the Other line and harms the Wei on the Self line, which is meeting a clash where there is a combination. Only in divining a lawsuit and an illness is meeting a clash where there is a combination welcome: combining, the matter surely succeeds; meeting a clash, calamity surely scatters; a matter about to be in danger is rescued, an illness about to end in death revives. The rest follow this. It also says: where a lucky spirit combines it must not be clashed, and where a fierce spirit combines a clash is welcome. One may perhaps follow this judgement.

> [!note] 按
> 「合处逢冲」一章的总句是：吉事逢合本来好，若在合的地方遇冲，事情将成而破。占婚、占官、占财、谋事都是这样，只有占官司和占病是例外：那两样要的是灾散，逢冲正好。这一条是占病那一句：病快要死了逢冲就活。「子冲应上午、害世上未」是子日冲午、又害未（子未相害）。这一句和这本书的「逢冲」一章（LY.ILL.DYTJ.28）一起，是冲在病里的两面：合处逢冲救病，静爻遇日冲叫暗动，吉凶都有。另外，天玄赋的「月解交重灾渐退」（LY.ILL.TXF.05）也是把灾冲散一类。
>
> *Note:* The general statement of the chapter 'a clash where there is a combination' is that a lucky matter meeting a combination is good, but if it meets a clash in the place of the combination, the matter about to succeed is broken. It is so in divining marriage, office, wealth and planning a matter, and only in divining a lawsuit and an illness is it the exception: those two want calamity to scatter, and meeting a clash is just right. This entry is the sentence on illness: an illness about to end in death lives on meeting a clash. 'Zi clashes with the Wu on the Other line and harms the Wei on the Self line' is the Zi day clashing with Wu and also harming Wei (Zi and Wei harm each other). This sentence together with the chapter of this book on 'meeting a clash' (LY.ILL.DYTJ.28) gives the two sides of a clash in illness: a clash where there is a combination saves the illness, and a still line meeting a day clash is called a hidden movement, with both luck and ill. The Tianxuan Fu's 'when the Month resolves and the Day moves heavily calamity fades' (LY.ILL.TXF.05) is also of the kind that scatters calamity.
