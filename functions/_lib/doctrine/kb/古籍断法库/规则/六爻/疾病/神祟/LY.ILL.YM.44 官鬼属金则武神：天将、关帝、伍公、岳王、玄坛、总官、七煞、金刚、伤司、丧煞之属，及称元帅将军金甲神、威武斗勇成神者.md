# 官鬼属金则武神：天将、关帝、伍公、岳王、玄坛、总官、七煞、金刚、伤司、丧煞之属，及称元帅将军金甲神、威武斗勇成神者

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[易冒]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 官鬼属金的，就是武神。像天将、关帝、伍公、岳王、玄坛、总官、七煞、金刚、伤司、丧煞这一类；有的地方称作元帅、将军、金甲神的，以及靠威武善斗成了神的，都可以类推。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* If the Pressure line is Metal, it is a martial spirit, as with the Heavenly Generals, Emperor Guan, Lord Wu, Prince Yue, the Dark Altar, the Chief Official, the Seven Killings, the Vajra, the Injuring Office and the Mourning Killing; and those locally called marshals, generals and golden-armoured spirits, and those who became spirits by martial might and fighting courage, can all be reasoned by analogy.

> [!warning] 打架
> 要不要把金鬼指成关帝、岳王这样的具体某位神：易冒举天将、关帝、伍公、岳王等作例子，但同一章的末尾说「必实指鬼神而泥之则诬」（LY.ILL.YM.63）；增删卜易的李我平点名批评《易冒》指定关岳（LY.ILL.ZS.25），觉子也认为金官指为关公岳公等武神不对，武神有封有未封、各地不同，要问病人许愿未酬的是哪座庙（LY.ILL.ZS.24）。易冒自己也只把这些当作例子，不当作定论。
>
> *Where they disagree:* Whether a Metal ghost should be pinned to a particular god such as Emperor Guan or Prince Yue: the Yi Mao gives the Heavenly Generals, Emperor Guan, Lord Wu, Prince Yue and the rest as examples, but at the end of the same chapter says 'to point insistently at a particular ghost or spirit and cling to it is to make a false claim' (LY.ILL.YM.63); Li Woping of the Zengshan Buyi names the Yi Mao in criticising the pinning to Guan and Yue (LY.ILL.ZS.25), and Jue Zi also holds that calling a Metal Pressure line Guan Gong or Yue Gong is wrong, since martial spirits are enfeoffed or not and differ from place to place, and one should ask which temple the patient vowed to without redeeming (LY.ILL.ZS.24). The Yi Mao itself takes these only as examples, not as conclusions.

> [!note] 按
> 「元坛」是「玄坛」避讳的写法，下面属火那一条里也有。这份名单里的神名，书里一个也没有解释，这里按字面译或音译，不作认定。「方隅有称元帅将军金甲之神」是说各地方对武神的称呼不同，按当地的称呼归到金里。
>
> *Note:* 'Yuan Altar' is the taboo-avoiding form of 'Xuan Altar' (the Dark Altar), and appears again in the Fire list below. Names of gods and goddesses in the lists are given by transliteration or by a plain rendering; the book does not explain any of them, and none is identified here beyond what the book says. 'Locally called marshals, generals and golden-armoured spirits' means that the names given to martial spirits differ from place to place, and whatever the local name, it is filed under Metal.
