# 总论八宫值鬼诀(下)：鬼发巽宫是东南镇海施相公、花园树木神道、长女、缢死疯疾腿折腰跎鬼；离宫是南方火部诸神（南斗六司、火德星君、五显灵官、灶神）、中女、目疾焚烧鬼；坤宫是西南土皇、坟墓、已故母亲老妇、虚黄鼓胀鬼；兑宫是正西佛天口许、伤神、少女、缺唇师巫鬼，加朱雀动是自己罚誓或他人咒咀天曹

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[易林补遗]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 巽宫：病患在东南，是冒犯了镇海的施相公、花园树木的神道，以及长女和上吊死的、疯疾的、腿折的、驼背的鬼。离宫：祸从南方起，是火部的诸神——南斗六司、火德星君、五显灵官、香头灶神这一类，还有中女以及眼盲、火烧这类的鬼，要赶紧酬愿、焚香。坤宫：西南方来，是冒犯了土皇使人不安，或者是坟墓上的缘故，和已故的母亲、年老的妇人，以及面色虚黄、腹胀如鼓的鬼。兑宫：祸落在正西，是许过佛、天的愿，也犯了伤神和少女，以及缺唇的、师巫这类的鬼；再加朱雀发动，不是自己发过誓、赌过咒，就是别人在天曹那里咒了他。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* Xun: the illness lies in the southeast, and it is an offence against Lord Shi who pacifies the sea, the spirits of garden trees, and the eldest daughter and ghosts of the hanged, of madness, of broken legs and of bent backs. Li: the misfortune rises in the south, and it is the gods of the Fire Department, the likes of the Six Departments of the Southern Dipper, the Fire Virtue Star Lord, the Five Manifest Spirit Officials and the incense head and Kitchen God, and also the middle daughter and ghosts of blindness and burning, and the vow should be redeemed quickly and incense burned. Kun: it comes from the southwest, and it is an offence against the Earth Emperor that brings unrest, or a matter of graves, together with the late mother and old women and ghosts of pale-yellow emptiness and drum-like swelling. Dui: the misfortune stains the due west, and it is a vow made to Buddha or Heaven, and it also offends the injuring spirit and the youngest daughter and ghosts of the harelipped, teachers and shamans; and if the Vermilion Bird moves too, then if it is not one's own sworn oath it is someone else cursing at the Heavenly Bureau.

> [!note] 按
> 这是「总论八宫值鬼诀」的下半（巽、离、坤、兑），体例同上一条（LY.ILL.EL.44）。「路冲」是路上冲撞；「腰跎」按字面译作弯腰，疑是「腰驼」；「麻疯」即麻风。离宫的歌诀「离家鬼发」的「家」按上下文是「宫」；它只有两个分句，一半歌诀一半解说，这里都当歌诀译。解说里「神撤南方有犯」的「撤」字费解，按「南方的神有所冒犯」的大意译；「香头灶神」按字面译作「香头、灶神」，「香头」可能是管香火的人，也可能是香火神，书里没有解释。「萱堂」是母亲（与上一条的「椿庭」相对）；「虚黄鼓胀」是面色萎黄、腹胀如鼓一类的病，和本章「土鬼」（LY.ILL.EL.34）的「膨胀虚黄」相同。兑宫最后「加朱雀动」是说朱雀也动：朱雀主口舌，所以是罚咒发誓一类的口舌事，不是自己发过誓，就是别人在天曹面前咒过他；和黄金策「朱雀咒诅口舌」（LY.ILL.HJC.96）、易冒「朱雀离神词讼囹圄冤鬼」（LY.ILL.YM.59）同一路。巽宫的「缢死、疯疾」是黄金策的巽（LY.ILL.HJC.95），「镇海、园林、长女」是易冒的巽（LY.ILL.YM.57），这里两边都收。这一段里神、鬼、星煞的名字，是吴地的民间神祇和星煞，书里没有解释，这里按字面译或音译，不作认定。
>
> *Note:* This is the second half of the 'general discourse on the eight palaces' (Xun, Li, Kun, Dui), in the same form as the previous entry (LY.ILL.EL.44). 'Lu chong' is a clash on the road; 'yao tuo' is translated literally as a bent waist and is probably 'yao tuo' meaning hunchback; 'ma feng' is leprosy. In the Li verse 'Li jia gui fa' the 'jia' (house) is 'palace' from the context; it has only two clauses, half verse and half commentary, and both are translated as verse here. In the commentary the 'che' in 'shen che nan fang you fan' is hard to understand, and is translated in the general sense 'there is an offence against a god in the south'; 'xiang tou zao shen' is translated literally as 'the incense head and the Kitchen God', and the 'incense head' may be a keeper of the incense fire or an incense spirit, which the book does not explain. The 'Xuan hall' is the mother (as opposed to the 'Chun ting' of the previous entry); 'pale-yellow emptiness and drum-like swelling' is an illness of a sallow face and a belly swollen like a drum, the same as the 'swelling and pale-yellow emptiness' of this chapter's 'Earth ghost' (LY.ILL.EL.34). The last 'with the Vermilion Bird moving' in Dui means the Vermilion Bird also moves: the Vermilion Bird governs the mouth and tongue, so it is a matter of oaths and curses, and if it is not one's own oath it is another's curse before the Heavenly Bureau; it is on the same path as the Huangjin Ce's 'the Vermilion Bird is curses and quarrels' (LY.ILL.HJC.96) and the Yi Mao's 'the Vermilion Bird is the spirit of Li, lawsuits and the grievous ghost of prison' (LY.ILL.YM.59). The Xun's 'hanged and madness' is the Huangjin Ce's Xun (LY.ILL.HJC.95) and 'pacifying the sea, gardens and groves and the eldest daughter' is the Yi Mao's Xun (LY.ILL.YM.57); here both are taken in. Names of gods, spirits and stars in this passage are given by transliteration or plain rendering; they are cults and stars of the Wu region that the book does not explain, and none is identified here beyond what the book says.
