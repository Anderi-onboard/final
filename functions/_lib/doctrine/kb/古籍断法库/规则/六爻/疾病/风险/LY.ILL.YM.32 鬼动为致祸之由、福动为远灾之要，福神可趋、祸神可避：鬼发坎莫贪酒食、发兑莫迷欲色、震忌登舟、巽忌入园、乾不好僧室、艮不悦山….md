# 鬼动为致祸之由、福动为远灾之要，福神可趋、祸神可避：鬼发坎莫贪酒食、发兑莫迷欲色、震忌登舟、巽忌入园、乾不好僧室、艮不悦山居、坤弗食牛、离因嗜炙；子孙发于是宫则反视

六爻 · 疾病 · 风险 · 出处 [[易冒]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 所以鬼动是招来祸的缘由，福动是远离灾的要领；福神可以趋近，祸神可以躲开。于是鬼在坎宫发动，就不要贪酒食；在兑宫发动，就不要迷恋欲色；震宫忌坐船，巽宫忌进园子，乾宫不可以喜欢去僧房，艮宫不可以喜欢住山里，坤宫不吃牛，离宫是因为爱吃烤炙的东西。注说：如果是子孙在这一宫发动，这个地方反而有利有福，和前面说的正好相反；乾宫利于僧堂，坤宫利于田地，坎宫适合江海，艮宫适合山，离宫乐于文章，兑宫爱好酒，震宫喜欢舟车，巽宫喜欢园囿。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* So a moving ghost is the cause that brings misfortune, and a moving Output is the key to keeping calamity away: the fortune spirit can be sought and the misfortune spirit avoided. When the ghost moves in Kan, do not be greedy for wine and food; in Dui, do not be lost in desire and lust; for Zhen avoid boarding a boat, for Xun avoid entering a garden; for Qian one must not favour monks' rooms, for Gen one must not delight in mountain dwellings; for Kun do not eat beef, and for Li it is from a fondness for roasted food. The note says: if Output moves in the same palace, that place is advantageous and blessed, the reverse of what was said before; Qian favours the monks' hall and Kun the fields, Kan suits river and sea and Gen the mountain, Li delights in writing and Dui in wine, Zhen likes boats and carriages and Xun gardens and parks.

> [!note] 按
> 这一条是上一条（LY.ILL.YM.30）的反面：上一条从病因往回推，这一条向前讲怎么躲。同一个宫，鬼动时忌的东西，子孙动时反而是宜的东西——坎宫鬼动忌酒食，子孙动时宜江海；乾宫鬼动不可好僧室，子孙动时利僧堂。「福神可趋」的「福神」在这里是子孙（Output），「祸神」是鬼。「离因嗜炙」的「嗜炙」是爱吃烤炙的东西，取火象；「坤弗食牛」取坤为牛。
>
> *Note:* This entry is the reverse of the last (LY.ILL.YM.30): that reasons back from the cause of the illness, and this looks forward to how to avoid it. In the same palace, what is avoided when the ghost moves is what is favoured when Output moves: with the ghost moving in Kan wine and food are avoided, and with Output moving Kan suits river and sea; with the ghost moving in Qian one must not favour monks' rooms, and with Output moving Qian favours the monks' hall. The 'fortune spirit' in 'the fortune spirit can be sought' is Output, and the 'misfortune spirit' is the ghost. 'Fondness for roasted food' in Li takes the image of Fire, and 'do not eat beef' in Kun takes Kun as the ox.
