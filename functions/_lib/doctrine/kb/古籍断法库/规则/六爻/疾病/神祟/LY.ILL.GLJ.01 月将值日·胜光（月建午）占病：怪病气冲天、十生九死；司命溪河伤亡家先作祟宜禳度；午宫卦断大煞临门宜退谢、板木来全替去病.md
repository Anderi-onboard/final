# 月将值日·胜光（月建午）占病：怪病气冲天、十生九死；司命溪河伤亡家先作祟宜禳度；午宫卦断大煞临门宜退谢、板木来全替去病

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[断易鬼灵经]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 月建落在午、叫胜光的时候占病：病来得古怪，气往上冲，十个里有九个要死，活下来的也拖得很久；心里结着热，气息紊乱急促，又添疼痛，病情难说。作祟的是司命、溪河一类的神，和横死的亡魂、家里的祖先，要禳解超度。后面「午宫卦断」另有一段：大煞到了门上，适合送走它并还愿答谢；社司和朝鬼拖累病人好几年；板木来齐了替他，病就去了，病人慢慢平安痊愈。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* When the Month falls on Wu, named Shengguang, divining illness: the illness is strange, the breath surging up to the sky, nine of ten die and the one who lives lingers on; heat knots in the heart, the breath is disordered and hurried, pain is added, and the illness is hard to describe. The ones at work are spirits such as Siming and the streams and rivers, the dead by injury, and the household ancestors, and rites of averting and deliverance are called for. A further passage after 'the verdict for the Wu-palace hexagrams' says: the Great Killer is at the door, and it is fitting to dismiss it and give thanks; the Altar Official and the Court ghosts drag on for years; when the wooden boards have come, complete, to stand in for him, the illness goes and the sick person gradually finds peace and cure.

> [!note] 按
> 这一条是「论十月将值日要决」十二首里的第一首。十二首的格式一样：先是小标题（某将值日，括号里写月建），然后是一首七言诗，前半写这个月份占病的症状和作祟的是谁，中间插一句「某宫卦断」，后半是这一宫的卦怎么断、怎么禳解。标题写的是「十月将」，实际列了十二将，从午（胜光）排起，到巳（太乙）为止，每将对着一个月（午=五月……巳=四月）；标题说「值日」，括号里写的却是「月建」「某月之事」，所以这里按月份读，书没有说日和月是怎么换算的。  这一首里的几个词书里都没有解释，译文只照字面：「司命」是掌管命运的神（民间也指灶神，书里另有「灶司」）；「伤亡」是横死、受伤而死的亡魂，「家先」是家里的祖先，「大煞」是一种凶煞，「社司」是社（土地神坛）的神，「朝鬼」不知所指；「板木」不知是什么，按上下文像是做法事时用来替身的木板（下一首说「板木未全凶怪梦」，板木没备齐就凶），这是读法，不是书里写的。「气舛急」的「舛」字按上下文读作气息紊乱，「怪病」的「怪」是说病来得古怪、像撞了什么，不是一个病名。「午宫卦断」书里没有说是指哪些卦，译文只写「午宫的卦」。
>
> *Note:* This is the first of the twelve pieces of the 'essentials of the month-generals ruling the day'. All twelve have the same form: a small heading (so-and-so rules the day, with the Month in brackets), then a seven-character verse whose first half gives the symptoms of this month's illness and who is at work, with a line 'the verdict for such-and-such palace hexagrams' in the middle, and a second half on how hexagrams of that palace are judged and how the matter is averted. The title says 'ten month-generals' but twelve are listed, from Wu (Shengguang) to Si (Taiyi), each answering to a month (Wu = the fifth month ... Si = the fourth month); the heading says 'rules the day' but the brackets speak of the Month and of 'the matters of such-and-such month', so the verses are read here by month, and the book does not say how day and month are converted.  Several words in this verse are not explained in the book and are rendered only literally: 'Siming' is the god in charge of fates (in popular religion also the Kitchen God, and the book elsewhere writes 'the Kitchen Official'); 'the dead by injury' are the souls of those who died violently or by wounds, 'the household ancestors' are the family's dead, 'the Great Killer' is a kind of fierce killer, 'the Altar Official' is the spirit of the village altar, and what the 'Court ghosts' are is not known; what the 'wooden boards' are is not known either, and in context they look like boards used as stand-ins in a rite (the next verse says 'when the wooden boards are not complete it is ominous, with strange dreams'), which is a reading and not what the book writes. In 'the breath is disordered and hurried' the character rendered 'disordered' is read from context; the 'strange' of 'strange illness' says the illness comes in an uncanny way, as if something had been run into, and is not the name of a disease. The book does not say which hexagrams 'the Wu-palace hexagrams' are, and the translation says only 'the Wu-palace hexagrams'.
