# 通玄妙论·逢冲暗动：占家宅暗动吉则暗中有补、凶则暗中有害；占病亦随其所遇之神而定其吉凶

六爻 · 疾病 · 动变 · 出处 [[卜筮全书]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 通玄妙论讲逢冲暗动，末一句是：占病也是随着所遇到的那位神，来定它的吉凶。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* The Tongxuan Miaolun on a line stirred by a clash ends with: in divining illness, too, good or ill is fixed by the spirit that is met.

## 《卜筮全书·通玄妙论·五、逢冲暗动》原文

> [!quote]+ [[卜筮大全]] 第 2845 行 · Bushi Quanshu (Complete Book of Divination), "Tongxuan Miaolun, no. 5: a clash that stirs a still line", base text
> | 原文 | 译文 | English |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 家宅遇之， | 占家宅遇到暗动， | In divining a house, meeting a stirred line, |
> | 吉则暗中有补， | 吉就暗中有补益， | if good there is a hidden help, |
> | 福已至而不知； | 福已经来了却不知道； | fortune has already come and is not known; |
> | 凶则暗中有害， | 凶就暗中有伤害， | if bad there is a hidden harm, |
> | 祸欲萌而未觉。 | 祸刚要萌生却没有察觉。 | and misfortune is about to sprout and is not noticed. |
> | 占病亦随其所遇之神， | 占病也是随着所遇到的那位神， | In divining illness too, it goes by the spirit that is met, |
> | 而定其吉凶也。 | 来定它的吉凶。 | and its good or ill is fixed. |

^q1

> [!note] 按
> 「暗动」是静爻被日辰冲动（断易天机、卜筮大全这一节开头都有定义：六爻安静，不遇冲则不动，日辰相冲，名曰暗动）。「所遇之神」按上下文读是被冲动的那一爻是什么神，就按那一爻是吉还是凶来断，书里没有另外注。断易天机里同一节对占病写得具体（LY.ILL.DYTJ.28：吉则病人有默佑，凶则阴隙所致），卜筮大全这一本只有这一句概括，一本具体、一本笼统，不矛盾。这一句前面的家宅一联是同一节里的，一并带着，让「占病亦随」有所指。
>
> *Note:* A 'stirred' line is a still line set moving by the Day branch clashing it (both the Duanyi Tianji and the Bushi Daquan define it at the head of this section: if all six lines are still and there is no clash, nothing moves; if the Day clashes a line, it is called stirred). 'The spirit that is met' is read from the context as: whatever kind of line has been stirred, judge by whether that line is good or bad; the book gives no separate note. The same section in the Duanyi Tianji is more specific about illness (LY.ILL.DYTJ.28: if good, the sick person has a silent protector; if bad, it comes from a hidden crack), while this copy in the Bushi Daquan has only this general sentence; one is specific and the other general, and they do not conflict. The couplet on houses before this sentence belongs to the same section and is carried along so that 'in divining illness too' has something to refer to.
