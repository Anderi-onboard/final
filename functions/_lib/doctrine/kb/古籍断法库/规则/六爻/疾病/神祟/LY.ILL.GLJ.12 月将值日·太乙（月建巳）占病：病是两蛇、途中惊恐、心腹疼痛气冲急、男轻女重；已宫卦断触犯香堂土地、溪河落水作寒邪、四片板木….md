# 月将值日·太乙（月建巳）占病：病是两蛇、途中惊恐、心腹疼痛气冲急、男轻女重；已宫卦断触犯香堂土地、溪河落水作寒邪、四片板木皆有、病人火里出莲花

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[断易鬼灵经]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 月建落在巳、叫太乙的时候占病：太乙日得的病，是两条蛇作祟，在路上受了惊吓，招来祸邪；心腹疼痛，气往上冲、急促，男的轻、女的重，手脚还有血光之灾，有咒咀和口舌。「巳宫卦断」那一段说：冲犯了香堂和土地，溪河、落水的鬼作寒邪；四片板木都齐备了，病人就像火里长出莲花。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* When the Month falls on Si, named Taiyi, divining illness: an illness on a Taiyi day is the work of two snakes, a fright on the road has drawn in misfortune and evil; the heart and belly ache with the breath surging and hurried, light in a man and heavy in a woman, and there is also the calamity of bloodshed on the hands and feet, with curses and quarrels. The passage 'the verdict for the Si-palace hexagrams' says: he has offended the incense hall and the Earth God, and the ghosts of streams and rivers and of the drowned make cold evil; once the four wooden boards are all there, the sick person is like a lotus coming out of the fire.

> [!note] 按
> 格式和名词见 LY.ILL.GLJ.01。标题括号里「月建值已」「已宫卦断」的「已」是「巳」的误写；「脚手血光炎更有」的「炎」读作「灾」（这本书里别处常把「灾」写成「炎」），是读法；「神广咒咀」的「神广」不知所指，译文照字面写；「口牙」读作口舌（争吵），同书别处也有「寻君起口牙」的说法；「男轻女重最交加」是说男的轻、女的重、轻重夹杂，书里没有展开；「四片板木皆有已」的「已」字不通，按上下文读作「已经」，整句读作四片板木齐备了（同 GLJ.01 的「板木来全」），是读法；「火里出莲花」是说在绝境里转好，书里没有注。十二首到这一首为止；原文这一行末尾还连着下一部分的标题「精校断易鬼灵经」，不收。
>
> *Note:* For the form and the terms see LY.ILL.GLJ.01. In the heading's brackets 'the Month falls on Si' and 'the Si-palace' the printed character is a misprint of 'Si'; the 'calamity' of 'the calamity of bloodshed on the hands and feet' is read from a character printed 'inflamed' (this book often writes 'calamity' that way), which is a reading; 'Shenguang' is not known and is written literally; 'quarrels' is the book's 'mouth and teeth', which the book uses elsewhere too, as in 'come looking for you and stir up mouth-and-teeth'; 'light in a man, heavy in a woman, all mixed together' says light in a man, heavy in a woman, light and heavy mingled, and the book does not expand it; the last character of 'the four wooden boards are all there' does not make sense as printed and is read from context as 'already', so that the whole line reads that the four boards have been made ready (as with 'the wooden boards come, complete' in GLJ.01), which is a reading; 'a lotus coming out of the fire' says that things turn for the better out of a desperate pass, and the book gives no comment. The twelve verses end here; the end of this line in the original runs into the heading of the next part, 'the carefully collated Duanyi Guiling Jing', which is not quoted.
