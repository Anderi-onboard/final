# 三丘五墓杀：春丑夏辰秋未冬戌为三丘、却与五墓对宫取，病人作福也难留；此占病忌，或有吉神解救方免

六爻 · 疾病 · 生死 · 出处 [[断易天机]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 春天是丑、夏天是辰、秋天是未、冬天逢戌，这是三丘；五墓要取三丘的对宫。病人就是作福（祈福）也难留住。这是占病所忌，只有得吉神来解救才能免去。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* In spring Chou, in summer Chen, in autumn Wei and in winter meeting Xu: these are the Three Mounds; the Five Tombs are taken from the opposite palace. The sick person, though he makes offerings for fortune, can hardly be kept. This is a taboo in divining illness, and it is escaped only if a lucky spirit comes to rescue.

> [!note] 按
> 这首「三丘五墓杀」的头四句和卜筮大全、易林补遗（LY.ILL.EL.51）里的三丘五墓歌是同一首，后面两句「此占病忌，或有吉神解救方免」是这里加上的。三丘是春丑、夏辰、秋未、冬戌；五墓「对宫取」，也就是三丘的对冲：春未、夏戌、秋丑、冬辰（这个排法见 LY.ILL.EL.51）。「病人作福也难留」和天玄赋的「二杀俱动，病人作福也难留」（LY.ILL.TXF.08）是同一句。神煞月令表里也有「三丘」「五墓」两行（丑丑丑辰辰辰未未未戌戌戌；未未未戌戌戌丑丑丑辰辰辰），那两行的断语是「坟墓崩，隐为崇凶」「坟墓崩陷凶」，不是专说病，这里不收。
>
> *Note:* The first four lines of this 'Three Mounds and Five Tombs killing' are the same verse as the Three Mounds and Five Tombs verse in the Buyi Daquan and the Yilin Buyi (LY.ILL.EL.51), and the last two lines, 'this is a taboo in divining illness, and it is escaped only if a lucky spirit comes to rescue', are added here. The Three Mounds are Chou in spring, Chen in summer, Wei in autumn and Xu in winter; the Five Tombs are 'taken from the opposite palace', that is the clash of the Three Mounds: Wei in spring, Xu in summer, Chou in autumn and Chen in winter (for this layout see LY.ILL.EL.51). 'The sick person, though he makes offerings for fortune, can hardly be kept' is the same sentence as the Tianxuan Fu's 'when both killings move, even if the sick person makes offerings for fortune he can hardly be kept' (LY.ILL.TXF.08). The table of spirits and killings by month also has two rows 'Three Mounds' and 'Five Tombs' (Chou Chou Chou Chen Chen Chen Wei Wei Wei Xu Xu Xu; Wei Wei Wei Xu Xu Xu Chou Chou Chou Chen Chen Chen), and the judgments of those two rows are 'the tomb crumbles, hidden it is an ominous curse' and 'the tomb crumbles and sinks, ominous', which are not specially about illness and are not taken here.
