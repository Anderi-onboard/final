# 六十四卦课·占病逢乾、姤、观、节、革、丰、师、大畜、履、渐：病人痊愈、占病无妨、占病即愈、疾病皆除、疾病渐好、疾病大好、官司疾病皆无妨

六爻 · 疾病 · 生死 · 出处 [[文王金钱课]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 文王金钱课的六十四卦课（原有的二十六卦）里，这十个卦的断辞说病会好：乾「病人痊愈」，姤「占病无妨」，观「占病即愈、疾病皆除」，节「疾病口舌消除安」，革「官司疾病皆无妨」，丰「疾病渐好」，师「疾病大好」，大畜「疾病口舌渐渐磨」，履「疾病皆除」，渐「官司疾病俱无妨」。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* In the sixty-four hexagram readings of the Wenwang Jinqian Ke (the twenty-six that the book originally has), the verdicts of these ten hexagrams say that the illness will mend: Qian, 'the sick are cured'; Gou, 'divining illness: no harm'; Guan, 'divining illness: cured at once, all illness removed'; Jie, 'illness and quarrels are cleared and peace comes'; Ge, 'lawsuit and illness are both without harm'; Feng, 'illness gradually mends'; Shi, 'illness mends greatly'; Daxu, 'illness and quarrels are gradually worn away'; Lü, 'all illness is removed'; Jian, 'lawsuit and illness are both without harm'.

> [!warning] 打架
> 文王金钱课和周易尚占（LY.ILL.ZSZ.02 至 ZSZ.06）都是按卦名断病，可是同一个卦两本说的，有四个相反或相左：姤，周易尚占说「病者难安」，这里说「占病无妨」；丰，周易尚占说「病人沉滞」，这里说「疾病渐好」；师，周易尚占说「病人迟瘥」，这里说「疾病大好」；履，周易尚占说「弗占疾病」，这里说「疾病皆除」。乾、观、革、渐、大畜这五个两本一致说病会好；节，周易尚占没有讲病。另一方面，观、大畜在别的书的占病凶卦名单里（LY.ILL.CA.03、DYTJ.15、TXF.06 等），观卦两本都说病好，正好和那些名单相反（见 LY.ILL.ZSZ.02）。书里没有裁决。
>
> *Where they disagree:* The Wenwang Jinqian Ke and the Zhouyi Shangzhan (LY.ILL.ZSZ.02 to ZSZ.06) both judge illness by the name of the hexagram, but for the same hexagram the two say things that are opposed or at odds in four cases: Gou, the Zhouyi Shangzhan says 'the sick will find it hard to be at peace' while here it says 'divining illness: no harm'; Feng, the Zhouyi Shangzhan says 'the sick one is weighed down and lingering' while here 'illness gradually mends'; Shi, the Zhouyi Shangzhan says 'the sick recover late' while here 'illness mends greatly'; Lü, the Zhouyi Shangzhan says 'do not divine illness' while here 'all illness is removed'. Qian, Guan, Ge, Jian and Daxu are five in which the two agree that the illness will mend; for Jie the Zhouyi Shangzhan does not speak of illness. On the other side, Guan and Daxu are in other books' lists of ominous hexagrams for illness (LY.ILL.CA.03, DYTJ.15, TXF.06 and so on), and for Guan both books say the illness mends, exactly against those lists (see LY.ILL.ZSZ.02). The books do not decide.

> [!note] 按
> 这部书原文只有二十六卦（乾宫、坎宫、艮宫各八卦，震宫两卦），每卦有「象曰」（七言四句）、「诗曰」（四言四句）、「断曰」（四言四句）三项，讲病的话有的在断曰里，有的在诗曰或象曰里；这里把含病话的那一两行收在一起，每一段的卦名写在引文标签上。每卦的题目是「卦名（课名）」，如「天山 遁（浓云蔽日）」。几处原文的写法：「天风垢」的「垢」是姤的误写，「天泽覆」的「覆」是履的误写，「风鸣歧山」按字面应是「凤鸣岐山」，原文如此，不改。「和课」（革、大畜、节、坎等处）应是「合伙」（补录部分同一位置写作「合伙」），译文按「partnership」译。「旱苗得雨芪堂堂」的「芪」意思不明，译文按上下文读作苗长得挺拔。「交易得魁」的「魁」是第一名。「疾病口舌渐渐磨」的「磨」是磨掉、慢慢消磨。这些卦的断辞每句都是四个字的断语，没有说用神、世应、动变。
>
> *Note:* In the original this book has only twenty-six hexagrams (eight each for the Qian, Kan and Gen palaces and two for the Zhen palace), each with three items, 'the image verse' (four lines of seven characters), 'the poem' (four phrases of four characters) and 'the verdict' (four phrases of four characters); the words on illness are in the verdict in some, and in the poem or the image verse in others, and here the one or two lines containing words on illness are taken together, with the name of the hexagram written on each quotation label. The heading of each hexagram is 'name of hexagram (name of reading)', as in 'Heaven over Mountain, Dun (Thick Clouds Hide the Sun)'. For some original spellings: the 'gou' of 'Heaven over Wind, Gou' is a miswriting of Gou, the 'fu' of 'Heaven over Marsh, Fu' is a miswriting of Lü, and 'the Wind Cries on Mount Qi' should literally be 'the Phoenix Cries on Mount Qi'; all are as in the original and are not changed. 'He ke' (in Ge, Daxu, Jie, Kan and elsewhere) should be 'partnership' (the supplemented part writes 'partnership' in the same place), and the translation reads 'partnership'. The 'qi' of 'parched seedlings get rain, qi stately' is not clear in sense, and the translation reads it from the context as the seedlings standing tall. The 'kui' of 'trade takes first place' is the first place. The 'worn away' of 'illness and quarrels are gradually worn away' is rubbed off, slowly ground down. In these hexagrams every sentence of the verdict is a four-character phrase, and nothing is said of the Subject, the Self and Other, or moving lines.
