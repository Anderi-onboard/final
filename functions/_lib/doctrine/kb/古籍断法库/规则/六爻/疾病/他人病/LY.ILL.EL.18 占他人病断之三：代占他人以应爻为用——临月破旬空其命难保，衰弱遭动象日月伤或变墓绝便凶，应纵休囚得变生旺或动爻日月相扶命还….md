# 占他人病断之三：代占他人以应爻为用——临月破旬空其命难保，衰弱遭动象日月伤或变墓绝便凶，应纵休囚得变生旺或动爻日月相扶命还有救；生应为元神宜动、克应为忌客宜空；鬼临应上病必难痊；卦身不可临月破、不可成墓绝之乡

六爻 · 疾病 · 他人病 · 出处 [[易林补遗]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 替别人问，看应爻，如果临月破最难逃过；遇冲遇克，人难救；逢旺逢生，病必消；生应的元神宜发动，克它的忌象怕交重；卦身有气，还是吉；应位遇上官鬼，祸必招来。注说：替别人占病，应作用爻，如果临月破、旬空，他的命难保；应如果衰弱，又遭动象或日月来伤、或应变成墓绝，就主凶危；应即使休囚，如果变为生旺、或有动爻和日月相扶，命还有救。凡是生应的爻是元神，宜动；克应的象是忌客，宜空。鬼临在应上，病一定难痊。再察卦身，也不可临月破，又不可成墓绝之乡；卦身或墓、或绝于月建之中，都是凶兆。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* In asking for another, look at the Other line; if it sits in Month-break, it is hardest to escape; meeting clash or control, the person is hard to save; meeting strength or generation, the illness surely dissolves; the Support that generates the Other should move, and the adversary image that controls it fears moving; if the Body has qi it is still auspicious; if Pressure meets the place of the Other, misfortune is sure to be invited. The commentary says: in divining another's illness, the Other is the Subject line; if it sits in Month-break or Void, his life is hard to keep; if the Other is weak and is wounded by a moving image or by the Day and Month, or the Other changes into a tomb or Extinction, it means danger; though the Other is resting or trapped, if it changes into generation or strength, or a moving line and the Day and Month support it, life can still be saved. Whatever generates the Other is the Support and should move; whatever controls the Other is the adversary guest and should be Void. With the ghost on the Other, the illness is surely hard to cure. Then examine the Body: it must not sit in Month-break, nor become a place of tomb or Extinction; if the Body is in a tomb or in Extinction under the Month, both are ominous signs.

> [!note] 按
> 「占他人之病，应作用爻」是易林补遗的定则（见 LY.ILL.EL.21 的最后两句：「占自己取世为用爻，占他人取应为用爻」）；但第 LY.ILL.EL.21 表里又按占的是谁另取用神（占父母取父母、占子取子孙……），两条并存：应爻是代占时的通用用爻，六亲各有自己的用神。原文在注的第一段「应如衰弱亦遭动象」处缺一个「若」字，不影响意思。这一条与黄金策「占他人：身世随官入墓不必忌」（LY.ILL.HJC.41）是同一路读法，也与易隐取用爻一条（LY.ILL.YY.03）的「代占以应爻为用」一致。
>
> *Note:* 'In divining another's illness, the Other is the Subject line' is the Yilin Buyi's standing rule (see the last two sentences of LY.ILL.EL.21: 'in divining oneself take the Self as the Subject line, in divining another take the Other'); but the table in LY.ILL.EL.21 also takes a Subject by the person divined (a parent takes the Resource line, a son the Output line, and so on), and the two stand together: the Other is the general Subject line when one divines for another, and each of the six relations has its own Subject. The original omits a word before 'the Other if weak' in the first part of the commentary, which does not affect the sense. It reads in the same way as the Huangjin Ce's 'in divining another, the Body and Self following the Pressure line into a tomb need not be feared' (LY.ILL.HJC.41) and agrees with the Yi Yin's entry on taking the Subject line, 'divining for another takes the Other line as the Subject' (LY.ILL.YY.03).
