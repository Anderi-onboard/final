# 还赛解厄：以福神为主、用象为凭；因灾酬愿寻福德、凶吉再辨用爻是否得令；用弱忌兴灾未退、忌衰用旺患能消；父母动克子则洪恩浅、财动生官则邪魅招；世旺子强官受制则祭神之后别无妖、赛神后保平安、解厄后无咎

六爻 · 疾病 · 神祟 · 出处 [[易林补遗]]

> [!abstract] 规则(整理,不是原文)
> 酬愿还赛、解厄，以福神（子孙）为主，以用象为凭。因为遭了灾要酬愿，就找福德；吉凶还要辨用爻得不得令。用爻弱而忌神兴，灾没有退；忌神衰而用爻旺，病患能消。父母发动克子孙，恩典浅薄承受不住；财爻发动生官鬼，邪魅就招来了。世爻旺、子孙强、官鬼受制，祭神之后就再没有妖祟；世是自己，旺则病必痊愈，子孙是福，强则压住鬼祟——这个强了，别的就必弱，福旺鬼将除，赛神之后保平安，解厄完了没有咎，禳解就吉，请祷就没有殃。
>
> *Rule (our summary):* In redeeming vows and lifting calamity, Output (the fortune spirit) is the main line and the Subject image is the ground. Because of a calamity one redeems a vow, so look to Output; for ill or good one must still distinguish whether the Subject line has gained or lost the season. With the Subject weak and the Adversary rising, the calamity has not retreated; with the Adversary weak and the Subject strong, the affliction can dissolve. If Resource moves and controls Output, the great grace is shallow and cannot be borne; if Wealth moves and generates Pressure, evil spirits are invited. With the Self strong, Output strong and the Pressure line held in check, after sacrificing to the gods there is no further evil; the Self is me, and if strong the illness will surely be cured; Output is fortune, and if strong it suppresses the ghostly haunting, and where this is strong the rest must be weak: the fortune strong, the ghost is about to be removed; after giving thanks to the gods peace is assured, when lifting the calamity is done there is no blame, and exorcism then is auspicious and petitioning and praying has no misfortune.

> [!note] 按
> 章首小标是「以福神为主，用象为凭」——福神就是子孙。这一章说的是「酬愿还赛、解厄」这类事：遭了灾或病去还愿、谢神，怎么看能不能成。这一章的判据是本书常见的一套：子孙（福神）强、官鬼受制，还赛就吉；父母克子孙、财生官，则不吉。「用弱忌兴」「忌衰用旺」和本书前面的用神、忌神读法一致（LY.ILL.EL.22：忌神旺发患多年）；「世旺则病必安痊，子强则压制鬼祟」是把世爻当作自己、子孙当作能压鬼的福。原文「惟子卦无凶，寻福德占爻则吉」的「子卦」按「子孙一卦」译，读法没有把握；「邹眉」是「皱眉」；「无青咎」的「青」字费解，疑是「责」（无责咎），这里保留原字并标出；「洪恩」是大恩（上天的恩典）。这一章没有说「还赛要不要杀牲」，和上一节（LY.ILL.EL.54）的立场并不冲突。 这一段里神、经、法事的名字，书里没有解释，这里按字面译或音译，不作认定。
>
> *Note:* The chapter's subtitle is 'Output (the fortune spirit) is the main line and the Subject image is the ground' — the fortune spirit is Output. This chapter is about redeeming vows and giving thanks and lifting calamity: how to tell, when one redeems a vow or thanks the gods after a calamity or illness, whether it will succeed. Its criteria are a set common in this book: with Output (the fortune spirit) strong and the Pressure line held in check, redeeming the vow is auspicious; with Resource controlling Output and Wealth generating Pressure, it is not. 'Subject weak and Adversary rising' and 'Adversary weak and Subject strong' are consistent with the book's earlier reading of the Subject and the Adversary (LY.ILL.EL.22: the Adversary strong and moving makes an affliction of many years); 'the Self strong and the illness will surely be cured, Output strong and it suppresses the ghostly haunting' takes the Self as oneself and Output as the fortune that can press down the ghost. In the original 'as long as Output has no ominous sign, look to the divined line where Output sits', 'zi gua' (translated 'Output') is read as the Output hexagram, and the reading is not certain; 'zou mei' is 'zhou mei' (knitted brows); the 'qing' in 'wu qing jiu' is hard to understand and is probably 'ze' (blame), and here the original character is kept and marked; 'hong en' is great grace (heaven's grace). This chapter does not say whether redeeming a vow calls for slaughter, and this does not conflict with the position of the previous section (LY.ILL.EL.54). Names of gods, spirits, scriptures and rites in this passage are given by transliteration or plain rendering; the book does not explain them, and none is identified here beyond what the book says.
