02 · How to ask

The question does half the work. Ask well and the reading has something to hold on to.

An oracle answers the question you actually put to it — not the one you meant. A little care here is the difference between a reading that lands and one that talks past you. Four small habits, and you’ll never need them again.

1
Bring the one that’s keeping you up

Not the safest question — the real one. The figure has the most to say about the thing you actually keep circling back to. Ask that.

2
Say it plainly, then give it a spine

One clear sentence, then a line or two of context: who’s involved, what’s at stake, the fork you’re standing at. Not a diary — just enough that the reading has something to bite on.

3
Ask about a movement, not a fixed fact

“Should I take the offer?” reads far better than “will it rain Tuesday?” The instrument reasons about direction, timing and choice — give it a decision, and it comes alive.

4
One honest cast, then sit with it

Re-casting the same question hoping for a kinder answer only buys you noise. Ask once, in good faith. If the answer stings, that’s often the part worth reading twice.

03 · What comes back

A figure is a map of forces — not a verdict, and rarely a single one.

What returns is not a yes-or-no and not a fortune-cookie line. It is the shape of your situation: where the energy sits, which line is moving, which way the whole thing is tilting. Reading it is a small skill — here is the whole of it.

What it points to

The figure describes structure and motion — a configuration of forces and where they’re heading. Read it as terrain and weather, not as a lottery number. It tells you the lay of the land and which way the wind is blowing; the walking is still yours.

Read it more than once

A good reading is layered. The same figure holds several true readings at once, depending on which thread of your life you lay it against — work, a person, your own nerve. Hold it up to each. The right one usually recognises itself.

Ambiguity is the honesty

When a reading holds more than one meaning, that isn’t the instrument hedging — it’s refusing to flatten a complicated life into a slogan. A single, tidy answer to a genuinely open question would be the lie. Take the plural view as a gift: it’s telling you the door is still open.

04 · The one rule that outlives all the others

A good omen is not a finish line.

卦后并非万事大吉 · 仍需努力,自强不息

A favourable figure tells you the wind is with you — it does not row the boat. An unfavourable one names the cost early, while you can still afford to pay it. Either way the outcome is not delivered to you; it is earned. The oracle can show you the grain of the wood. You still have to do the carving — strive on, without ceasing. That has been the discipline behind this instrument for twenty-six centuries, and it is the one thing we’d ask you to carry out of here.

The figure is cast by the old logic

The six lines and the hexagram they form are produced by a faithful casting engine — the same causal method, untouched, that dynasties reasoned by. No model chooses the figure. Chance and structure do, exactly as they always have.

Claude finds the words — and only the words

The reading is written by Claude, held to the figure that was cast. The hexagram decides what may be said; the model finds a clear, human way to say it. It never overrules the casting to tell you what you’d rather hear. Ancient structure, modern voice — and an honest line between the two.
